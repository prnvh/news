## The claim

The last several years validated a simple bet: more compute, more data, larger models. That bet bought us fluency, generality, and surprise. But every empirical curve has a shape. We are now paying more for each increment, waiting longer for each iteration, and discovering new classes of failure the old recipe cannot solve.

The next phase will not be defined by a single knob. It will be defined by the quality of our data, the alignment of our objectives, the structure of our models, and the sharpness of our evaluation.

## The context

Compute costs have risen faster than Moore's law. Training runs that once took weeks now take months. Meanwhile, easy data is gone, model performance plateaus on many benchmarks, and real-world reliability lags behind demo-day glow.

At the same time, the world got more demanding. We need models that reason over long horizons, act safely in novel situations, and earn trust. That is a different optimization problem.

## The technical core

The path forward is a portfolio, not a single lever.

- Richer data: fewer tokens, higher information. Curation, diversity, and freshness over volume.
- Better objectives: beyond next-token prediction—learn to plan, verify, and adapt.
- World models: internal simulators to compress experience and evaluate interventions.
- Targeted reasoning: allocate compute where uncertainty or stakes are highest.
- Sharper evaluation: measure what matters, close the gap between tests and reality.

## The strongest objection

Is scale still the surest path? Sometimes. For broad capability floors, scale remains powerful. But as an exclusive strategy, it is brittle: expensive, slow to ship, and increasingly misaligned with the risks we face.

## What comes next

We need institutions and incentives that reward durable progress over leaderboard movement. That means open benchmarks, reproducible science, careful release practices, and a culture that prizes understanding.

The frontier will not be won by the team with the biggest cluster. It will be won by the teams that build the right systems—and know when not to build.
