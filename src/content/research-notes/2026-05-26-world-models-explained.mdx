## The result

Hafner et al. train an agent with a world model that predicts the next latent state and reward given the current latent state and action. The model is learned from raw pixels via a compact latent space, but it is trained to minimize prediction error in that space, not to reconstruct images. With this setup, the agent achieves state-of-the-art performance across a suite of continuous-control tasks, often with fewer samples than prior approaches.

## The core idea

Instead of learning a policy directly in the real environment, the agent learns a compact latent model of how the world evolves. It then uses this model to imagine many possible futures and choose actions that lead to high expected returns. Crucially, the model is trained on next-step prediction in latent space, avoiding the brittleness of pixel-level losses.

## Why it matters

This work shows that next-token prediction—applied to sequences of latent states and actions—can be a powerful general-purpose engine for control and planning. It narrows the gap between predictive modeling and decision-making, and points toward a unifying framework where agents reason, plan, and act in the same learned space.

## Limitations

Performance depends on the quality of the latent space and can degrade with very long horizons or highly stochastic environments. The approach also requires careful balancing of imagination, planning, and reality to avoid compounding model error.

## My take

This paper is a foundational step toward agents that think in models, not just policies. The elegance is in its simplicity: learn dynamics by predicting the next latent token, then plan in that space. Future work will need to scale this to richer modalities and longer horizons while keeping the latent world grounded.
