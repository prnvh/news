## 01. The problem

AGI is not a single invention waiting behind a finish line. It is a moving target defined by tasks, institutions, and risk models that change as systems improve.

## 02. Major approaches

The current landscape includes scale-first systems, world models, tool-using agents, symbolic-neural hybrids, automated evaluation, and infrastructure work that makes larger experiments possible. Each path carries a different assumption about what intelligence requires.

## 03. World models

World-model work asks whether systems can learn useful internal simulations: representations that let them predict consequences, plan actions, and reason beyond surface tokens.

## 04. Scaling paradigms

Scaling still matters, but it is not one thing. Data quality, architecture, inference compute, hardware supply, and training stability all shape whether additional compute becomes capability.

## 05. Evaluation

Measurement is now a frontier problem. The field needs evaluations that resist saturation, expose hidden brittleness, and track open-ended capability without turning into leaderboard theater.

## 06. Open questions

The hardest questions remain coupled: how to align systems, understand their internal representations, govern deployment, and build institutions that can respond faster than the technology changes.

## 07. Reading path

Start with capability evaluation, then read world-model work, then study alignment and governance. The useful path is not a ladder; it is a loop through technical evidence and institutional reality.
