Moore's Law is usually described as the observation that the number of transistors on a chip doubled on a regular cadence. The deeper point is that computing became cheaper, denser, and more capable because semiconductor manufacturing kept compounding.

This draft will separate the historical observation from the engineering reality: lithography, power, memory, packaging, accelerators, and the economics of modern fabs.
